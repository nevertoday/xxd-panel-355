## Panel-specific overlay

The transformed design is a straight-down view of one flat, clean, endless ground in the material that covers the largest area of the photograph, with no horizon and nothing else. A person, animal, building or object becomes a few large forms readable overhead, complete, small as a grain, with space above and a longer shadow. A scene keeps only its most representative object. One short word, language unfixed and with no preset title, crosses the middle a little low, clear of both edges, with empty ground above and below, and nothing touches the midline. Strokes are pressed grooves with softly raised edges, one side lit by low raking light and the other in shallow shadow, so light alone makes them dimensional. The last stroke drags into a long trail that loops one large teardrop, and the subject stands on it. The field is that one ground colour, grooves a step darker and shadows darkest, the only colour two or three tiny points on the subject. The word is a quiet rounded geometric sans-serif, Latin lowercase, closed by one large dot a step darker than the ground; any Chinese is Simplified. It names this moment from a visible detail, as its metaphor. Reject a full-scene copy, a templated poster, an eye-level or oblique landscape, printed rather than pressed letters, a large subject, extra colours, and a title that names the object, says "again", "still waiting" or "not yet", adds a number, rule or label, copies a sign or printed text, or becomes a stock phrase or a reflection.

These overlay rules add to `references/soldier-runtime.md`. They never replace the source brief or the family runtime contract.

Write every selected final PNG directly inside one fresh task directory under `~/Desktop/xxd/xxd-panel-355/` (or the explicit `--out` root). Use collision-safe filenames; do not create source, mode, or size subdirectories and do not generate an automatic contact sheet.
